\section{Grid-Cap Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{sec:grid_cap_results}

The grid-cap strain parameter $s \in \{0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00\}$ scales the
hard power limit $P_{\max}$ relative to the nominal value used in the main
benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:main_results}).  At $s = 1.00$ the cap equals
$n_{\text{ports}} \times 6\,\text{kW}$, matching the primary experiments; at
$s = 0.25$ the cap is tightened to one quarter of that level, severely
restricting simultaneous charging throughput.  All other parameters—port
count, BESS capacity, solver, seed set—are held fixed.

Each cell reports statistics over 30 independent seeds ($n = 30$).  Tables are
organised by tariff structure (fixed / dynamic) and BESS availability.  A
$\dagger$ symbol marks cells where at least one seed required the LP
infeasibility fallback (must-serve constraints relaxed); elevated mean solve
times confirm this.

\subsection{Net Profit under Dynamic Tariff}
\label{subsec:gc_dynamic}

Tables~\ref{tab:gc_dynamic_bess_profit}--\ref{tab:gc_dynamic_nobess_profit}
present the dynamic-tariff sweep ($1.5\times$ spot buy price) with and without
the on-site BESS.  Absolute profits are substantially lower than the fixed
tariff—approximately 28\,\% of fixed-tariff values—because the $1.5\times$
markup compresses the gross margin.  The controller ranking is preserved in
both BESS configurations, but the LP's marginal advantage over
\texttt{max\_charge} collapses to near zero when the BESS is absent
(Section~\ref{subsec:gc_dynamic_nobess}).

even without the BESS (Table~\ref{tab:gc_fixed_nobess_profit}).

The structural explanation is straightforward.  Under the dynamic tariff,
EV-customer revenue is always $1.5\times$ the grid buy price, so net profit
per unit of energy is a fixed 33\,\% margin regardless of \emph{when} that
energy is delivered.  Without BESS the LP cannot exploit spot-price variation
to arbitrage across time, and coordination across ports adds no revenue
because charging one vehicle sooner does not change the aggregate energy cost
relative to charging it later.  Consequently, the LP degenerates to the same
greedy solution as \texttt{max\_charge}: saturate available power on every
present vehicle at every timestep.

\subsection{Summary of Grid-Cap Sensitivity Findings}
\label{subsec:gc_summary}

The grid-cap sensitivity experiments yield four principal findings:

\paragraph{Finding~1 (Linear profit scaling).}
Net profit scales approximately linearly with the grid-cap multiplier~$s$
across all port sizes and tariff types
(Table~\ref{tab:gc_profit_scaling}).  This confirms that revenue is
dominated by energy throughput, which itself is bounded by $P_{\max}$.

\paragraph{Finding~2 (LP advantage is robust to cap tightness).}
The LP controller (\texttt{milp\_h12}) consistently outperforms
\texttt{max\_charge} at every strain level.  The absolute advantage is
slightly larger at tighter caps ($+3.12$\,€ at $s=0.25$ vs.\ $+1.96$\,€
at $s=1.00$ under the fixed tariff, aggregated across port sizes), indicating
that coordinated scheduling adds more value when the power budget is scarce
(Table~\ref{tab:gc_lp_advantage_bess}).

\paragraph{Finding~3 (BESS value increases under tight caps).}
The BESS provides the largest percentage profit gain at the tightest cap:
$+6.6\%$ at $s=0.25$ versus $+1.2\%$ at $s=1.00$ for \texttt{milp\_h12}
with 3 ports under the fixed tariff (Table~\ref{tab:gc_bess_gain}).  Under
tight caps, BESS discharge acts as a supplement to constrained grid power,
enabling higher charging throughput than grid power alone would permit.

\paragraph{Finding~4 (LP advantage requires BESS under dynamic tariff).}
Without the BESS, the LP's net-profit advantage over \texttt{max\_charge}
collapses to sub-cent under the dynamic tariff across all port sizes and all
strain levels (Table~\ref{tab:gc_lp_advantage_nobess}).  Under the dynamic
$1.5\times$ markup, margin is a fixed fraction of energy delivered regardless
of timing, so the LP's temporal scheduling adds no incremental value when
storage is absent.  This is in stark contrast to the fixed-tariff case, where
the LP retains a meaningful advantage over \texttt{max\_charge} even without
BESS through better per-port coordination.

\paragraph{Finding~5 (Solver remains real-time feasible).}
Even at the tightest cap and largest station size (12 ports, $s=0.25$),
mean per-step solve times remain below 230\,ms—well within the 5-minute
(300{,}000\,ms) timestep budget—despite occasional infeasibility-retry
overhead (Tables~\ref{tab:gc_compute} and~\ref{tab:gc_dyn_nobess_compute}).
This confirms real-time tractability of the LP across the full range of grid
constraint severity and BESS configurations tested.
